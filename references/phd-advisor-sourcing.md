## Part 1 — Advisor research method (adapted from the wiki's Q&A)

### Where to find information (ranked by reliability)
| Source | Use for | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lab / department official site | Open positions, admissions, funding | Often stale |
| Professor homepage | Research direction, current students, openings | Last update may be ancient |
| Google Scholar / DBLP | Whether the group ACTIVELY publishes in your area (last 3–5 yrs) | — |
| Current students (cold email) | Mentoring style, push level, internship policy, graduation destinations | Most valuable, hardest to get |
| Reddit / GradCafe / 小红书 / Zhihu | Leads, interview experience | Discover leads here, never decide here; "hot on 小红书" = oversaturated |

### Match check — three dimensions
1. **Direction match:** can you read the titles of their recent papers? Is it a direction you'd do for
   2–5 years? Do you like the direction itself, not just its prestige?
2. **Style match:** strong-guidance+push+resources vs hands-off+freedom; engineering-heavy vs
   paper-driven. Decide first whether you want training / resources / freedom / title / graduation —
   rarely all at once.
3. **Goal match:** industry-bound → check internship support, alumni destinations, time for interview
   prep; research-bound → check stable output, training pipeline, senior students, whether the advisor
   helps with ideas.

### Verify the group BEFORE applying — cold email current students
Ask: mentoring frequency, group-meeting frequency, internship support policy, master/PhD graduation
requirements, group atmosphere, whether seniors mentor juniors.
**Signal:** a student who replies with specifics = good sign; no reply or "can't discuss" = treat the
group as a risk (communication may be strained). "Life is long; advisors are many — don't volunteer into
  a pressure cooker."

### Should you do a PhD at all — self-test
Worth considering a PhD if: willing to research one concrete problem long-term; accept unstable output
for years; don't mind writing papers, running experiments, repeated failure.
Cool down if: mainly avoiding the job market; just like the title; exhausted and seeking a buffer.
A PhD is not a fancier master's; it is a long, uncertain, self-driven training.

### Anti-pattern
Don't be driven by anxiety: emailing professors because others do, applying because others apply,
switching research interest to whatever is trending. Decide your own target list first; channels like
小红书 surface leads but concentrate competition ("a professor trending on 小红书 is genuinely crowded").
