## 中文原文（verbatim）

①要 match 不要 interest
我导说，现在最害怕学生第一份邮件就说"对我的课题很感兴趣…" 所以我当时申请的时候，申请老师会带着我把目标老师最近的论文和项目先过一遍，看到 future work 也会记下来，再去找自己的科研经历能接在哪里。 申请老师还专门给我做了个小表格（当时觉得好麻烦，后面写陶瓷信， SOP 的时候真的救命）。

②要 ability 不要 passion
这个也特别常见。比如"我一直关注 XX ，希望博士阶段继续深入研究"，到这里其实还是比较泛。 可以再具体一点：你到底发现了什么问题？为什么现在的研究还没解决？你想从哪里切进去？ 不用现在就把博士论文想得明明白白，但至少别让教授看完只知道你"很有热情"。

③面试别太被动，要主动问，不要只会附和
我帮导师筛材料时发现，他打分最低的往往是全程" yes "" I agree "的学生。用他的原话："我要的是能主动提问、一起聊研究的伙伴。" 后来申请老师带着我做 mock interview 才懂，导师不是考标准答案，而是看能不能和你聊研究。如果你觉得一个方法不错，但自己发现它有局限，就直接说，再问一句"您怎么看？"我当时还把模拟面试录了音，一句句改：哪里啰嗦、哪里没例子、哪里解释过头。

④要 follow up ，不要频繁催促
很多人把 follow up 做成了催结果，邮件发出去两天就追问，一周催好几次。但导师忙，出差、开会常见，回邮件本来就没那么及时。催得太勤，只会被忽略，甚至留下急躁、不稳重的印象。 读博久了就明白， follow up 要讲分寸：隔一周礼貌问一次，并且 update 一下最新进度，别追着要结果。导师选人，不只看成绩，更看做事靠不靠谱。被拒往往不是能力差，而是细节没做好

## Key Takeaways (English)

1. **Match > Interest — the "paper + future work" method.**
   - Never open with "I'm very interested in your research." Every professor hears this.
   - Instead: (a) read the professor's recent 2–3 papers + current grants; (b) note their stated "future work" / limitations; (c) find **one specific overlap** with your own research experience; (d) write the email around that overlap.
   - Practical tool: build a spreadsheet before writing any email — columns: Professor | Recent Paper | Future Work / Gap | Your Matching Experience. This table becomes gold when writing cold emails AND SOPs.

2. **Ability > Passion — show the gap, not the enthusiasm.**
   - Weak: "I've been following XX and hope to continue this research in my PhD." (Generic, no signal.)
   - Strong: "I noticed that [method X] doesn't handle [specific problem Y]. I think [your approach Z] could be a starting point because [reason]. Here's what I've tried so far…"
   - You don't need a complete PhD proposal. But the professor should finish reading and think: "This person has identified a real gap and has a direction" — not just "This person is enthusiastic."

3. **Interview: be a research partner, not a yes-machine.**
   - Professors rank "always agreeing" students lowest. A professor's direct quote: "I want someone who can ask questions and discuss research together."
   - Tactics:
     - If you like a method but see a limitation → say so, then ask "What's your take on this?"
     - Don't just answer — **engage**. Treat the interview as a research conversation, not an exam.
     - Mock interview tip: record yourself, then review line by line — cut filler, add concrete examples, avoid over-explaining.

4. **Follow up with updates, not pressure.**
   - ❌ Emailing every 2 days asking "Any update?" — professors are busy (travel, conferences), this reads as impatient and unstable.
   - ✅ Follow up once a week, and **attach a brief progress update**: "Since my last email, I've [done X / read Y / reproduced Z]. Just wanted to keep you posted and check if there's any update on your end."
   - Core principle: professors evaluate reliability and judgment, not just grades. Many rejections come from details, not competence.

## Quick Reference — Email Cadence

| Timing | What to do | What NOT to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Send cold email (match-focused, not passion-focused) | Generic "I'm very interested" |
| Day 7 | Follow up #1 with a brief progress update | "Any update??" |
| Day 14 | Follow up #2 (final) — reiterate match + new update | Third follow-up with no new info |
| Day 21+ | Move on. Send a polite thank-you if they replied; if no reply, stop | Keep emailing without new content |
