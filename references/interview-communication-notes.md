# Interview & Communication Notes — register calibration & presence

> Field notes from real coaching sessions (Chinese originals at the bottom). Opinion/experience notes,
> not facts. Load for: interview prep, behavioural stories, "tell me about your project" practice,
> founder-pitch rehearsal, SOP/CV narrative tone checks — or whenever the user asks how to talk about
> their background to different audiences （面试准备， 行为面试， 怎么讲自己的项目， 表达沟通）.

## Rule 1. Jargon calibration: match how niche your field is
- **Broad / not strongly technical industries** (product, audit, consulting, ops, general business roles):
  consciously embed the field's standard terminology while narrating your experience — fluency with these
  terms IS a credential that proves professional understanding. Examples: product → *product-market fit,
  user journey, retention curve*; audit → *existence and completeness testing, control effectiveness,
  substantive procedures*.
- **Niche / highly technical fields** (physics research, specialised biotech, deep hard-tech): do the
  OPPOSITE. Switch to plain English; make yourself understood. In niche domains the scarce skill is
  **translation** — explaining specialised work to smart generalists. Jargon here signals inability to
  communicate, not mastery.
- Decision rule: *Does my listener use this term every day?* Yes → use it (fluency signal). No → translate
  it (comprehension signal).
- Applies beyond interviews: SOPs (mixed admissions committee → translate); founder pitches (investors →
  translate; regulated-industry enterprise clients → use THEIR vocabulary); CV bullets (niche hiring
  manager → plain precise English; generalist HR screen → keep the standard keywords).

## Rule 2. Presence is part of the credential in front-office / client-facing roles
- Field observation: a Barclays UK S&T saleswoman was instantly identifiable as sales from her presence —
  speech pace, energy level, and structured logic all stood out. In S&T / sales / BD / fundraising /
  client-success roles, interviewers assess presence AS competence, not as a personality bonus.
- Prep for these roles: rehearse answers out loud, record yourself; check pace (faster, decisive openings),
  structure (answer-first / pyramid principle), and energy sustained across a full 30–60 min slot.
  Hedging and fillers cost more than in engineering interviews.
- Calibration: for quant/research/engineering tracks presence is still table-stakes (clear, confident) but
  the balance tilts to precision and depth — do not perform sales-energy in a research interview.

## Rule 3. Story mechanics (both registers)
- Every "tell me about X": one-line context → your specific action → quantified outcome → so-what
  (same structure as an Evidence Map entry).
- Place calibrated terms naturally inside the action; never dump them as labels.

## 中文原稿（verbatim）

1. 上周见到了一个在UK巴克莱做S&T的女生，那铺面而来的大E人气质真的让我一眼认定是做sales的人。无论是她的整体语速，还是给人的energy level，讲话的逻辑等等都非常非常standout。很有意思。

2. 在给一个startup做产品的人和在审计做audit的学生上课的时候，经常讲的话就是要把一些行业术语有意识的插入到讲自己经历的过程中。比如product market fit, user journey；existence and completeness testing, control effectiveness等等。但是在给一个牛津本硕学物理的学生上课的时候，我永远都在强调，换一个语言讲话，至少讲plain english。这是在于一个没有很多strong technical的行业里面，要下意识多用这样的词去"证明"你的专业理解程度。但是在一个已经非常niche的行业里面，尤其是甚至做的东西也不算是常见的物理or生物的时候，讲人话，让人听懂的能力就变得更加重要了。
