# Why the most capable young people are also the most exhausted

> Field note (Chinese original verbatim at the bottom). Load when: the user (or someone they know)
> is burning out despite being high-performing, or when framing the relationship between personal
> iteration speed and the social recognition / resource lag. This note reframes "越努力越幸运" by
> adding the *structural* explanation: when your iteration speed out-paces society's公允
> evaluation speed, you have to carry the load yourself — and that itself is a form of privilege.
> （年轻人最累， 迭代速度 vs 社会公允速度， privilege， 热血和意志力， 00后）。

## The observation
Some young people who are *so capable they bend the rules* share a common pattern:
- They are the strongest among their peers.
- They are also the most exhausted.

## The structural explanation (not the platitude)
The platitude: "越努力越幸运" — the harder you work, the luckier you get — is set aside.

The real pattern:
- Many 00后 work day and night, create relentlessly, run themselves ragged, and keep going on
  willpower and passion.
- The reason: **their personal iteration speed significantly exceeds society's 公允 speed for
  evaluating young people.**
- The identities, circles, resources, wealth, and other forms of *social freedom* and *social
  leverage* that society grants them **lag significantly behind** their core competitive power —
  their cognitive horizon, execution ability, etc.
- Therefore they can only **靠自己去猛猛干、猛猛熬夜** — carry the load themselves.

## The flip side — which is also true
Conversely: the fact that they have an iteration speed *significantly above* society's 公允 pace
is itself the result of **some resources society has *already* granted them in advance**.
This, too, is a form of **social privilege**.

## How this connects to the rest of the repo
- `career-mindset-adaptability.md` — the meta-skill of "responding to world change" often shows up
  precisely as this asymmetry: your cognition has moved on, but your social position hasn't
  caught up. The answer is not to slow down; it is to recognise the asymmetry and act accordingly.
- `founder-anti-hedging-principles.md` — the same structural asymmetry appears in founders: the
  company has outgrown its current institutional form, but the institutional form hasn't caught
  up. The answer is the same: don't hedge, commit to the direction your iteration is pointing.
- `career-oak-high-level-state-transition.md` — the Oak framing: what matters is the high-level
  state transition (your capability class), not the micro-step count (your hours logged). The
  exhausted cohort is often the one making the fastest high-level transitions; the fatigue is the
  cost of moving between "states" faster than the social map updates.
