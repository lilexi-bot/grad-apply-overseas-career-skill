My name is [Your Full Name], and I am applying for the Doctor of Philosophy program in [ECE / Computer Science] at [University Name] for the [Fall 2027] intake. My application ID (if available): [XXX].

I am writing to respectfully request an application fee waiver. I am a self-funded student from mainland China. As a prospective PhD applicant, I have invested heavily in preparing my research statement, recommendation letters, GRE/TOEFL examinations, and other application materials. The cumulative cost of applying to multiple PhD programs has created a substantial financial burden for me at this stage.

My research interest lies in [brief 1 sentence]. I have carefully reviewed the research work of Prof. [Name] in your department, and my background and research goals align closely with his group. I am highly motivated to pursue doctoral research at [University Name].

I understand that fee waivers are limited and granted on a case-by-case basis. I would greatly appreciate your consideration of my request. Please let me know if any supporting documents are required.

Sincerely,
[Your Full Name]

**美国 PhD 申请费 fee waiver 及模板分享**

美国 PhD 申请是个劳神伤财的事，基本上单个项目的申请费就 100 刀，贵的地区像加州要收 135 刀，将近一千块了，而申请季要报几十所学校，实在是一笔不菲的费用。基本上大部分项目都可以，屡试不爽——主包申请的 15 所学校中就有 12 所都给免了申请费。

**第一档 夯爆了**：哈佛普林斯顿之类的顶校，只需在申报系统的最后、交钱之前，勾选是否申请 fee waiver（类似这种简单操作），直接就免掉了。

**第二档 人上人**：耶鲁哥大等学校，在申请时对 fee waiver 有相关说明，需要在额外的 page 提交几百字经济情况说明，并提交银行流水（有的不需要），三四个工作日后可以 waive 掉。模板在图 2 自取。

**第三档 NPC**：部分学校在申请截止前一两个月有早鸟福利，规定日期之前提交可以免费；还有些私立学校的宣讲会可以参加，参加后可以免申请费。

**第四档 拉完了**：很多公立学校没有，只给本州学生 fee waiver，或者没有免费政策，像 UT Austin、威斯康星麦迪逊等，貌似红州公立基本都不给华人学生免费。

## English Reading Guide

US PhD applications cost roughly $100/programme (up to $135 in California, e.g. UC system); applying to 15+ schools means $1,500–2,000+ in fees alone. Most programmes will waive the fee on request — the reporter's empirical hit rate: 12 waivers out of 15 applications. Schools fall into four tiers of waiver accessibility:

### The four tiers

| Tier | School type | Mechanism | Effort |
|---|---||---|---|
| 1 — Auto checkbox | Top Ivies (Harvard, Princeton, etc.) | Check "request fee waiver" at the final payment step in the application portal; auto-approved | One click |
| 2 — Documented request | Yale, Columbia, etc. | Dedicated fee-waiver page: short (few-hundred-word) financial-hardship statement + sometimes bank statement; decision in 3–4 business days | Write statement, maybe upload docs |
| 3 — Early bird / events | Some programmes ("NPC") | Free submission before a cutoff 1–2 months ahead of deadline; or waiver after attending a private-school information session/virtual fair | Watch deadlines, attend sessions |
| 4 — Not available | Many public universities (UT Austin, UW–Madison, etc.) | Waivers restricted to in-state applicants or nonexistent; red-state publics reportedly rarely waive for Chinese international students | Budget full fee |

### Template logic (why it works)

The email template is short and contains exactly what admissions offices expect:

1. **Identity + programme + term + application ID** — make the file instantly locatable.
2. **Respectful direct request** — "respectfully request an application fee waiver," no apology, no dramatisation.
3. **Self-funded international applicant + cumulative burden** — one factual sentence: mainland China, self-funded, heavy spend on RS/LOR/GRE/TOEFL across many programmes. Hardship is stated with dignity, not pleaded.
4. **Merit hook** — one-sentence research interest + named professor match. This signals you are a serious PhD candidate, not a mass-mailer; fee waivers exist partly to remove barriers for strong applicants.
5. **Process awareness** — acknowledge waivers are limited/case-by-case; offer supporting documents. Makes the officer's job easy.

### Practical tactics

- **Apply early:** Tier-3 early-bird windows close 1–2 months before deadlines; build the waiver step into the application timeline.
- **Attend info sessions / graduate fairs:** private universities commonly distribute waiver codes after registration/attendance (virtual events count).
- **Ask before paying:** tier-1 checkbox appears only at the payment step — do not pay first and ask later.
- **Bank statement prep:** tier-2 may ask for one; have an English-labelled statement ready (a low-balance/savings statement is the point — it evidences hardship).
- **Budget tier-4 schools as full-fee:** assume publics like UT Austin/Wisconsin are non-waivable for international applicants; allocate the application list and budget accordingly (e.g., prioritise reach schools that waive freely).
- **GRE/TOEFL fee reduction** is a separate mechanism (ETS voucher) — not covered here, but worth checking for test-cost relief.

## English Key Takeaways

1. **Empirical waiver rate: 12/15.** Asking is cheap and usually works; never pay without checking the waiver path first.
2. **Four tiers:** checkbox auto-waiver (top Ivies) → documented hardship request (Yale/Columbia, ~3–4 day turnaround) → early-bird/info-session codes → publics with no international waiver.
3. **Template structure:** identity/app ID → direct request → self-funded + cumulative cost burden → research fit with named professor → acknowledge limited availability + offer documents.
4. **Tone:** factual and dignified; state hardship, don't perform poverty. The professor-match line reframes you as a wanted candidate.
5. **Timeline matters:** early-bird free-submission deadlines land 1–2 months before final deadlines; info-session codes require attendance before applying.
6. **Budget planning:** fees run $100–135 each; plan a 15-school budget around the expectation that red-state publics likely charge in full.
