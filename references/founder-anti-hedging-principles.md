# Stanford GSB Entrepreneurship — Three Anti-Intuitive Principles

> Field notes from Stanford Graduate School of Business Entrepreneurship course (Chinese original
> verbatim at the bottom). Load when: founder is stuck in planning / over-diversifying / clinging to
> "safe" incremental market entry / betting on technology alone. Cross-references the cold-start
> playbook (which is tactical); this file is strategic — it challenges the founder's framing before
> tactics kick in （斯坦福创业课， 创业反常识， 创业底层逻辑， One-Two Punch Fallacy， 创业没有中间地带， 黑科技未必赢）。

## Principle 1. Reject the "one-two punch fallacy" (小市场练手 → 大市场做大)
- Common belief: start in a small market to practice; once you make money, move to a big market.
- Reality: **small markets and big markets require entirely different talent, technology, and sales
  models.** You are not laying a foundation — you are building two completely different buildings.
- Correct move: **fix the Vision first, then go directly to the market worth being in.** The small-market
  warm-up creates a legacy stack that must be rebuilt later, not a learning shortcut.
- Use as a filter: if the founder's narrative says "we start in X then expand to Y" — ask what specific
  capabilities would transfer. If none, the plan is two startups, not one scaling.

## Principle 2. Startup has no middle ground
- In mature companies, compromise is an art. In startups, compromise is suicide.
- **All-in or pivot fast.** The worst state is "both this and that" — resources get diluted until
  nothing gets done.
- Use as a diagnosis: when progress stalls, check whether the team is running three initiatives that
  each deserve a full team at half-strength. Collapse to one, kill the others, or spin them out.

## Principle 3. Best technology does not necessarily win
- Google won on the **business model** (precision advertising), not the best search algorithm. McAfee
  won by exploiting IT departments' "laziness" and freemium psychology, not superior malware detection.
- If you have black-tech but don't understand human nature and Marketing, you will be taken apart by
  a less brilliant competitor who does.
- Use as a pitch check: every founder deck should answer "why do users actually switch?" in ONE
  sentence that references a human behaviour (laziness, fear, status, inertia), not a technical
  capability.

## How these three hang together
They are the same lesson at three scales:
- **Market choice** (P1): don't hedge with a "safe" first market — go straight to the one worth winning.
- **Resource allocation** (P2): don't hedge across initiatives — all-in on one, or pivot.
- **Product design** (P3): don't hedge on technology superiority — design for human behaviour first.

The meta-pattern: **hedging is the founder's default instinct, and it is the fastest path to
underperformance.** Conviction — not recklessness — is the scarce skill. (Note: this connects to the
founder "adaptive vs pushback" trait in `founder-fundraising-ecosystem.md` — founders who raise push
back on **core conviction** (which market, which bet, which audience) and adapt on **tactics**
(presentation, pricing, speed of iteration). This file defines what NOT to adapt on.)

## 中文原稿（verbatim）

Stanford首课：为什么绝大多数人创业会死？🤯 在斯坦福GSB的第一门Entrepreneurship课，教授一上来就把我的认知彻底粉碎了！💥 整理了3条最扎心的底层逻辑，建议所有想搞副业或创业的朋友反复读：

1️⃣ #拒绝“阶梯式冒险” (The One-Two Punch Fallacy) 很多人觉得：我先做一个小市场练手，等赚钱了再去做大市场。 教授：错！❌ 小市场和大市场需要的人才、技术、销售模式完全不同。你不是在打地基，你是在盖两栋完全不同的楼。定好Vision，直接去那个最值得去的地方！

2️⃣ 创业没有“中间地带” 在成熟企业，折中是艺术；但在Startup，折中就是自杀。 要么全仓买入，要么快速掉头。最怕“既要又要”，最后资源被稀释到什么都做不成。

3️⃣ 最好的技术未必会赢 Google赢在商业模式（精准广告），McAfee赢在利用了IT部门的“懒惰”和Freemium心理。 哪怕你有黑科技，不懂人性、不懂Marketing，一样会被降维打击。
